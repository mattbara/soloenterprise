# Startup to Enterprise: Key People & Scaling Playbook

> A data-feed document for workforce planning across company stages.

---

## Company Stage Definitions

| Stage | Headcount | Annual Revenue | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Startup** | 1 – 15 | Pre-revenue – £2M | Idea validation, MVP, first paying users, seed/Series A funding. Everyone wears multiple hats. |
| **Growth / Scale-up** | 15 – 80 | £2M – £20M | Product-market fit confirmed, repeatable sales motion, Series B/C. Processes start replacing heroics. |
| **Mid-size Business** | 80 – 300 | £20M – £100M | Multiple product lines or markets, departmental structure, Series D+ or profitable. Culture becomes intentional. |
| **Enterprise** | 300+ | £100M+ | Global operations, governance & compliance frameworks, IPO-ready or public. Systems run the business. |

---

## Stage Transition Signals

### Startup → Growth
- Monthly active users or revenue growing >15% month-over-month consistently
- Founders are the bottleneck on every decision
- Technical debt is slowing feature delivery
- Customer support is overwhelming the founding team

### Growth → Mid-size
- Need for middle management layers becomes unavoidable
- Revenue requires formal financial controls and forecasting
- Single points of failure in knowledge across the org
- Regulatory or compliance requirements increase with scale

### Mid-size → Enterprise
- Operating in multiple geographies or markets
- Board and investor governance demands dedicated functions
- Security, compliance, and data privacy require full teams
- Brand reputation becomes a strategic asset requiring protection

## Key Roles: The Full Journey

### Leadership & Strategy

| Role | Job Title | Description | Importance (1-10) | Stage Needed |
|------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Visionary Leader | **CEO / Founder** | Owns the vision, sets company direction, makes final calls on strategy, fundraising, and culture. In a startup this person IS the company — they pitch investors, close early deals, and set the tempo. As the company scales, they transition from doer to leader of leaders. | 10 | Startup |
| Operations Leader | **COO / Co-Founder (Ops)** | The person who turns the founder's vision into executable plans. Manages day-to-day operations, builds processes, and ensures the machine runs. Critical for founders who are product/tech-focused and need someone to handle the business side. | 9 | Startup |
| Technical Visionary | **CTO / Technical Co-Founder** | Translates the product idea into a technical architecture. Makes foundational technology decisions (stack, infrastructure, scalability approach) that the company will live with for years. In web/mobile startups, this person is arguably the most important early hire after the founder. | 10 | Startup |
| Product Strategist | **CPO / VP Product** | Owns the product roadmap and prioritisation. Bridges business goals with user needs and technical feasibility. At startup stage this might be the founder; by growth stage it needs to be a dedicated role. | 9 | Startup – Growth |
| Financial Steward | **CFO / VP Finance** | Manages cash flow, fundraising support, financial modelling, and unit economics. Startups can outsource this early, but by growth stage you need someone who deeply understands SaaS/product metrics and can guide profitability. | 7 | Growth |
| People Leader | **VP People / Head of HR** | Builds culture, hiring processes, compensation frameworks, and retention strategies. Culture is set in the first 15 hires — by the time you realise you need this role, you're often already behind. | 7 | Growth |
| Revenue Leader | **CRO / VP Sales** | Owns revenue targets and go-to-market execution. Builds the sales playbook, hires and manages sales teams, and drives pipeline. For product-led growth companies this role arrives later; for enterprise sales models, earlier. | 8 | Growth |
| Legal & Compliance | **General Counsel / Head of Legal** | Manages contracts, IP protection, regulatory compliance, employment law, and risk. Especially critical in regulated industries like gaming, fintech, or health. | 6 | Mid-size |
| Security Leader | **CISO / Head of Security** | Owns information security strategy, compliance certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001), incident response, and data protection. Increasingly important as you handle more user data. | 6 | Mid-size |

### Product & Design

| Role | Job Title | Description | Importance (1-10) | Stage Needed |
|------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Product Manager | **Product Manager** | Defines what gets built and why. Conducts user research, writes requirements, prioritises the backlog, and measures outcomes. The glue between business, design, and engineering. | 9 | Startup |
| UX/UI Designer | **Product Designer / UX Lead** | Designs the user experience and interface. Creates wireframes, prototypes, and design systems. In web/mobile products, design quality directly correlates with user retention and conversion. A bad UX will kill even a great idea. | 9 | Startup |
| User Researcher | **UX Researcher** | Conducts usability testing, user interviews, and data analysis to validate design decisions. Prevents the team from building what they assume users want vs. what they actually need. | 7 | Growth |
| Design System Lead | **Design Systems Engineer** | Builds and maintains the component library and design tokens that ensure consistency across the product. Becomes essential as the product and team grow and multiple squads are shipping UI. | 6 | Growth – Mid-size |
| Content Strategist | **Content Designer / UX Writer** | Crafts microcopy, onboarding flows, error messages, and in-app communication. The words in your product are part of the experience — this role dramatically improves usability and conversion. | 5 | Growth |

### Engineering

| Role | Job Title | Description | Importance (1-10) | Stage Needed |
|------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Frontend Lead | **Senior Frontend Engineer / Frontend Lead** | Builds the user-facing application. In web/mobile startups, this person (or small team) is responsible for everything the user sees and interacts with. Must be exceptional at React/React Native, TypeScript, performance optimisation, and responsive design. This is your product's face to the world. | 10 | Startup |
| Backend Lead | **Senior Backend Engineer / Backend Lead** | Architects and builds the server-side logic, APIs, databases, and integrations. Responsible for data integrity, security, and system performance. The quality of your backend determines your ability to scale. | 10 | Startup |
| Full-Stack Generalist | **Full-Stack Developer** | Versatile engineers who can work across the stack. Invaluable in startups where you can't afford specialists for every layer. They move fast, fill gaps, and keep velocity high. | 8 | Startup |
| Mobile Developer | **Mobile Engineer (iOS/Android)** | Builds native or cross-platform mobile applications. If mobile is a primary channel, this role is as critical as the frontend lead. Needs deep understanding of platform-specific patterns and app store requirements. | 8 | Startup (if mobile product) |
| DevOps / Platform | **DevOps / Platform Engineer** | Manages CI/CD pipelines, infrastructure-as-code, monitoring, and deployment automation. Without this role, your team spends increasing amounts of time fighting infrastructure instead of building product. | 8 | Startup – Growth |
| QA / Test Engineer | **QA Engineer / SDET** | Builds test automation frameworks, performs exploratory testing, and ensures quality gates before releases. Catches bugs before users do. The ROI of this role compounds over time as the codebase grows. | 7 | Growth |
| Data Engineer | **Data Engineer** | Builds data pipelines, warehouses, and ETL processes. Enables the business to make data-driven decisions. Without clean, reliable data infrastructure, your analytics and ML efforts will fail. | 7 | Growth |
| Engineering Manager | **Engineering Manager** | Manages engineering teams (typically 5-8 engineers). Handles 1:1s, career development, sprint planning, and cross-team coordination. The critical layer that lets the CTO stop being a bottleneck. | 8 | Growth |
| Principal/Staff Engineer | **Principal Engineer / Architect** | Provides deep technical leadership across the engineering org. Makes high-impact architectural decisions, mentors senior engineers, and ensures technical quality at scale. | 7 | Mid-size |
| SRE / Reliability | **Site Reliability Engineer** | Ensures uptime, performance, and reliability of production systems. Owns monitoring, alerting, incident response, and capacity planning. When your users depend on your product being always-on, this role is non-negotiable. | 7 | Mid-size |
| Security Engineer | **Application Security Engineer** | Conducts code reviews for vulnerabilities, penetration testing, and implements security tooling in the SDLC. Protects user data and company reputation. | 6 | Mid-size |

## The "First 10 Hires" Blueprint for Web/Mobile Startups

This is the order I would recruit if building a web or mobile product company from scratch:

| Hire Order | Role | Why This Order |
|------------|------|---------------|
| 1 | **CTO / Technical Co-Founder** | Someone has to build the thing. Your most critical early decision. |
| 2 | **Senior Frontend Engineer** | The product's face. Users judge your company by what they see and touch. |
| 3 | **Senior Backend Engineer** | The product's brain. APIs, data, logic — nothing works without this. |
| 4 | **Product Designer (UX/UI)** | Design-driven products win. Get this right early or accumulate UX debt. |
| 5 | **Product Manager** | Free the founder from backlog management. Someone needs to own "what" and "why". |
| 6 | **Full-Stack Developer** | Adds velocity and fills gaps across the stack. |
| 7 | **DevOps / Platform Engineer** | Automate deployments, set up monitoring. Stop deploying from laptops. |
| 8 | **Head of Growth / Marketing** | You've built it — now people need to find it. |
| 9 | **Customer Support Lead** | Users are arriving. Someone needs to listen to them and close the feedback loop. |
| 10 | **QA Engineer** | Quality compounds. Invest here before the codebase gets unwieldy. |

## Key Principles

1. **Hire builders first, managers later.** In a startup, everyone should be able to produce output directly. Management layers come when coordination costs exceed the cost of the managers themselves.

2. **Design is not a nice-to-have.** In web and mobile products, design IS the product. A beautifully designed MVP beats a feature-rich ugly one every time.

3. **Your first technical hires define your ceiling.** Bad early architecture decisions compound. Invest in senior engineers who've built at scale before, even if they cost more.

4. **Culture is set by the first 15 people.** Every subsequent hire either reinforces or dilutes that culture. Be intentional.

5. **Revenue solves most problems.** Hire people who understand that the purpose of the product is to generate revenue. Passion projects that don't monetise aren't businesses.

6. **Process should follow pain.** Don't implement enterprise processes in a startup. Add process only when the lack of it is causing visible problems.

7. **The best companies are talent magnets.** Your early team's reputation attracts your next wave of hires. A-players attract A-players; B-players attract C-players.
